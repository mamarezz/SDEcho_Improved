THEORETICAL FOUNDATION: SEQUENTIAL COUNTERFACTUAL SDECHO
========================================================

1. PURPOSE
----------

This project explains why two aggregate sequences differ.  A sequence is a
vector of group-level aggregates, such as mean annual salary for the experience
buckets 0-2, 3-5, 6-10, and 10-20 years.

The original SDEcho method discovers value-defined predicates whose matching
tuples are influential under removal.  The extension developed here asks a
second, explicitly distributional question:

    After making the two groups have the same prevalence of the first
    discovered predicate within the frozen intervention buckets, what
    predicate explains the remaining weighted sequence difference?

The method repeats discovery and balancing to produce an ordered explanation
path.  It is descriptive and counterfactual.  It does not identify causal
effects.


2. ORIGINAL SEQUENCES AND DISTANCE
----------------------------------

Let A be the source group and B the fixed reference group.  Let b index the
ordered sequence buckets.  For outcome Y, the observed bucket means are

    mu_A(b) = E[Y | A, b]
    mu_B(b) = E[Y | B, b].

The bucket-gap vector is

    delta_b = mu_A(b) - mu_B(b).

The project uses Euclidean sequence distance over the full declared index F:

    D_0^F = ||delta||_2
        = sqrt(sum_b (mu_A(b) - mu_B(b))^2).

The intervention policy freezes a subset S of material buckets from the
original gaps.  Weighted SDEcho removal uses distance D_t^S over S, while the
accepted balancing path uses D_t^F over every declared bucket.  Rows outside S
retain their base weights, so their coordinates remain visible but unchanged.

Euclidean distance is nonlinear: a global percentage is not an average of
bucket percentages, and large bucket gaps contribute quadratically.


3. WHAT SDECHO REMOVAL MEASURES
-------------------------------

A predicate P is a conjunction such as

    Country = USA

or

    Country = USA AND Education = Doctoral.

For a candidate P, SDEcho removes matching tuples from both groups, rebuilds
both sequences, and obtains D_{-P}.  Its removal reduction is

    R_remove(P) = 1 - D_{-P} / D_0.

The SDEcho ranking also penalizes predicates that cover large fractions of the
data.  With ordinary unweighted tuples,

    gamma(P) = (D_{-P} / D_0)
               * [1 + n_A(P)/N_A + n_B(P)/N_B].

Smaller gamma is stronger.  Removal is an influence diagnostic.  It can react
to prevalence differences, within-predicate outcome differences, outliers,
and the renormalization of the remaining means.  A large removal reduction is
therefore not automatically a compositional share of the original gap.


4. WHY REMOVAL AND REWEIGHTING DIFFER
-------------------------------------

Let I = 1{P} be the exact binary predicate event.  Within bucket b define

    q_gb       = Pr(I=1 | group g, b)
    m_gb1      = E[Y | group g, b, I=1]
    m_gb0      = E[Y | group g, b, I=0].

After deleting P from both groups, the residual bucket gap is

    r_b = m_Ab0 - m_Bb0.

After reweighting A so that its predicate prevalence equals B's prevalence,
while retaining A's conditional outcomes, the residual is

    c_b = (1-q_Bb)(m_Ab0-m_Bb0)
          + q_Bb(m_Ab1-m_Bb1).

Therefore

    c_b - r_b
      = q_Bb[(m_Ab1-m_Bb1) - (m_Ab0-m_Bb0)].

Removal and prevalence balancing agree only under special conditional-outcome
conditions.  Neither effect is universally larger.

If delta is the original gap vector and a transformation produces residual
delta^T, its signed distance effect is

    R_T = 1 - ||delta^T||_2 / ||delta||_2.

Interpretation:

    R_T > 0   the transformation moves the sequences closer;
    R_T = 0   the sequence distance is unchanged;
    R_T < 0   the transformation increases the sequence distance.

A negative value is a valid gap-amplifying result, not a mathematical error.

Writing a^T = delta - delta^T for the change in the gap vector, distance is
reduced exactly when

    2 * delta' a^T > ||a^T||_2^2.

Thus it is not enough for some coordinates to move.  The movement must point
sufficiently toward the target and must not overshoot by too much.

This direct comparison assumes that both transformations use the same
coordinate set.  Under the active material-bucket policy, SDEcho's exported
removal percentage is computed over S, whereas balancing path reductions are
computed over F.  Those percentages are different diagnostics and must not be
compared as if they shared a denominator.  Use an all-bucket run or separately
evaluate removal over F when a same-coordinate comparison is required.

Global categorical weighting also has an important limitation.  If X is
balanced globally with ratio p_B(X)/p_A(X), the weighted source law inside
bucket b is proportional to

    p_A(X | b) * p_B(X) / p_A(X),

which is generally not p_B(X | b).  This is why the iterative implementation
uses predicate-event-by-bucket constraints when it claims that a predicate has
been standardized across the sequence.


5. EVIDENCE FROM THE ORIGINAL REAL-DATA ANALYSIS
-------------------------------------------------

For ages 25-34 versus 35-44 in the Stack Overflow 2022 data, the original mean
salary sequences were approximately

    A = [135156, 131692, 173414, 205397]
    B = [123917, 194521, 212527, 197010]

with gap vector

    delta = [+11238, -62828, -39113, +8387]

and distance

    D_0 = 75324.9.

The first predicate was Country=United States of America.

Deleting USA rows from both groups gave distance 30113.7, a 60.02 percent
removal reduction.  The earlier full-Country reweighting intervention, applied
only to selected coordinates, gave distance 35623.5 and a 52.71 percent signed
effect.  Reweighting every coordinate under that global full-Country scheme
gave only about 6.16 percent because two bucket gaps became worse.

The large removal result was not pure Country composition.  In the 3-5 bucket,
USA prevalence and conditional mean salary differed substantially:

    USA share:       A 17.64 percent, B 26.68 percent
    USA mean salary: A 378632,       B 490669
    non-USA mean:    A 78809,        B 86733.

Removing USA rows eliminates much of this conditional upper-tail difference
from both groups.  Reweighting prevalence cannot do that because it retains
the source group's conditional USA salaries.

A direct diagnostic for the new method balanced the exact binary USA event
within every experience bucket.  It changed the distance from 75324.9 to
53883.5, a 28.47 percent reduction, with effective sample size about 96.2
percent of the source sample.  USA deletion still reduced the remaining
weighted distance by 44.11 percent.  Residual predicates were refinements such
as USA combined with education.  This illustrates why a previously balanced
predicate can remain influential under deletion: prevalence was standardized,
but conditional outcome heterogeneity remained.


The earlier implementation selected reweighted coordinates from the observed
outcome gaps.  Its threshold was

    max(0.18 * maximum absolute bucket gap, 10000)

combined with a 5 percent relative-difference requirement.  In the main data,
the threshold was about 11309.  The 0-2 gap of 11238 narrowly failed the strict
greater-than check, so only 3-5 and 6-10 were changed.  Restoring other
coordinates after weighting produced a hybrid sequence rather than the
sequence of one coherent weighted population.  The new implementation selects
the intervention buckets once from the original sequences and freezes that
set for the entire path.  It applies row weights inside those buckets and
leaves all other rows at their base weight, so every reported sequence comes
from one coherent weighted population.  The main configuration freezes 3-5
and 6-10; the synthetic correctness benchmark explicitly freezes all buckets.
Weighted SDEcho scores the same frozen bucket set that the calibration step can
change.  Its support, effective-support, and prevalence-penalty calculations
are restricted to rows in those buckets.  The reported balancing-path distance
still includes every declared bucket; unchanged coordinates therefore remain
visible in the residual.


6. LESSON FROM THE PREVIOUS SYNTHETIC BENCHMARK
------------------------------------------------

The earlier benchmark planted two conjunctive cells.  Each cell had source
share 5/100 = 5 percent and target share 20/130 = 15.38 percent.  Removing one
planted cell changed the other cell's relative shares to

    5/95   = 5.26 percent
    20/110 = 18.18 percent.

Deletion therefore amplified the remaining imbalance.  A single planted-cell
removal closed only about 30.24 percent of the gap even though the designed
component was larger.

The old reweighting operation was broader than deletion.  For a predicate such
as Advanced AND Metro, it ignored the values and balanced every SkillTrack by
Region cell.  That operation also moved background cells and leaked into the
second planted mechanism.  Its individual signed effects were approximately
46.63 percent and 41.87 percent.  They overlapped and could not be added.

Deleting the union of both planted cells left the designed 20 percent residual
and therefore gave exactly 80 percent reduction.  Full joint-attribute
alignment also approached 80 percent because that synthetic population had
complete support, identical conditional premiums, and stable cell proportions
across buckets.  The near equality was a property of the constructed data, not
a theorem connecting removal and reweighting.

This lesson motivates exact binary predicate-event balancing in the new
benchmark.  The intervention now corresponds directly to the value-defined
predicate selected at each iteration.


7. EXACT PREDICATE-EVENT BALANCING
----------------------------------

The intervention must preserve the meaning of the discovered predicate.  For
P = Country=USA, define

    Z_P = 1{Country=USA}.

The method balances USA versus non-USA.  It does not replace this event with
the complete distribution over every country.  Full-attribute alignment is a
broader intervention and answers a different question.

Because the output is bucketed, balance is imposed inside every frozen
intervention bucket.  Let q_Ab be A's current weighted prevalence and q_Bb be
B's fixed reference prevalence.  The single-constraint update in bucket b is

    factor for P-matching rows     = q_Bb / q_Ab
    factor for non-matching rows   = (1-q_Bb) / (1-q_Ab).

Both factors are necessary.  The expected update preserves total bucket mass
and makes the weighted source prevalence equal to the target prevalence.

The direction is A to B:

    B remains fixed;
    A is standardized to B's predicate-event distribution;
    A's conditional outcomes are retained.

For one binary constraint, the resulting one-sided counterfactual mean is

    mu_A^cf(b) = sum_z Pr_B(Z=z | b) E_A[Y | b, Z=z].

With several marginal constraints, cyclic calibration can also change the
within-event mixture.  The final mean is therefore the empirical weighted mean
of the calibrated pseudo-population, not a claim that the complete joint
distribution has been matched.

This direction answers: what would A's sequence look like under B's selected
predicate-event margins?  Reversing the direction can produce a different
path and is a sensitivity analysis, not the same estimand.


8. CUMULATIVE CALIBRATION
-------------------------

Suppose predicates P1 and P2 are selected sequentially.  A one-pass P2 update
can destroy the earlier P1 balance when the predicates are correlated.

At iteration t, weights are therefore recomputed from fixed base weights under
all accumulated constraints:

    Pr_A^w(P_j=1 | b) = Pr_B(P_j=1 | b)

for every selected predicate j <= t and every frozen intervention bucket b.

Cyclic calibration repeatedly applies the binary margin updates until the
maximum absolute balance error is below a fixed tolerance.  This preserves
earlier constraints.  It matches selected event margins, not necessarily their
complete joint distribution.  Matching the complete 2^t joint distribution
would become sparse rapidly.

Calibration can fail when the requested target margins are outside the source
support.  The implementation records the candidate and reason in the rejection
audit, then considers the next candidate in the configured pool.  It never
silently invents rows or clips weights; clipping would break exact balance.


9. WEIGHTED SDECHO ON THE RESIDUAL POPULATION
---------------------------------------------

Let w_gi^(t) be the row weights after t accepted balance steps.  The current
bucket mean is

    mu_g^(t)(b)
      = sum_{i in g,b} w_gi^(t) Y_i
        / sum_{i in g,b} w_gi^(t).

For a candidate predicate P, weighted removal sets matching weights to zero:

    w_gi^(t,-P) = w_gi^(t) * 1{i does not satisfy P}.

The calibration weights are not refitted while the candidate is scored.  At
the start of iteration t, let D_{t-1}^S be the distance over the frozen search
buckets and D_{t-1,-P}^S be that search distance after deleting P.  The
weighted SDEcho extension uses

    gamma_t(P)
      = [D_{t-1,-P}^S / D_{t-1}^S]
        * [1 + pi_A^t(P) + pi_B^t(P)],

where

    pi_g^t(P)
      = sum_i w_gi^t 1{i satisfies P} / sum_i w_gi^t.

All support, prevalence, and match-ESS quantities in this score are restricted
to rows in the frozen search buckets.  For candidates satisfying the same
support and nonempty-bucket rules, unit weights recover the ordinary
brute-force mean-based score.  Raw and active counts and effective sample size
are separate reliability diagnostics.  A candidate is not scored if its
removal leaves an empty weighted search bucket.


10. ITERATIVE ALGORITHM
----------------------

Input:

    source and target data;
    ordered buckets;
    outcome column;
    candidate attributes and maximum predicate order;
    candidate-pool size and value-enumeration limit;
    frozen intervention buckets;
    support and weight-quality thresholds;
    maximum number of iterations.

Algorithm:

    1. Initialize source and target weights to one.
    2. Build the initial weighted sequences and distance D_0.
    3. Run weighted SDEcho on the current pseudo-populations over the same
       frozen buckets that are eligible for intervention.
    4. Consider candidates in removal-score order within the configured pool.
    5. Exclude exact previously selected predicates and identical event masks.
    6. Add the candidate event to the accumulated constraint set.
    7. Recompute source weights from the base weights so that every selected
       predicate event matches the target within every intervention bucket.
    8. Reject a candidate if calibration is infeasible, balance does not
       converge, effective sample size is inadequate, weights are excessive,
       or the actual balance update does not materially reduce distance.
    9. Accept the highest-ranked feasible material candidate in that pool and
       use its weighted population as the next baseline.
   10. Repeat until a stopping rule is met.

SDEcho is the discovery mechanism.  Actual reweighting improvement determines
whether a proposed step is accepted.  This distinction prevents the method
from claiming progressive composition explanation when a deletion-helpful
predicate has a zero or negative balancing effect.


11. REPORTED EFFECTS
--------------------

For accepted step t, let D_{t-1}^F be the full-index distance before balancing
the new predicate and D_t^F the full-index distance after cumulatively
recalibrating all selected predicates.

The conditional step reduction is

    e_t = (D_{t-1}^F - D_t^F) / D_{t-1}^F.

These conditional percentages do not add.

The additive original-scale path increment is

    c_t = (D_{t-1}^F - D_t^F) / D_0^F.

This increment belongs to the selected path: it includes adding P_t and
recalibrating all accumulated margins.  It is not an intrinsic, order-free
contribution of P_t.

The cumulative reduction after T accepted steps is

    C_T = (D_0^F - D_T^F) / D_0^F
        = sum_{t=1}^T c_t.

Every iteration records both

    D_{t-1}^S:       search distance before selecting P_t;
    D_{t-1,-P_t}^S: search distance after SDEcho removal of P_t; and
    D_t^F:           full-index distance after balancing its prevalence.

This keeps deletion influence and compositional standardization visibly
separate.


12. STOPPING RULES
------------------

An individual candidate is rejected when calibration is infeasible, balance
does not converge, support or effective sample size is inadequate, weights are
excessive, or its actual balance update is not materially positive.  The search
then tries the next candidate in the configured pool.

Iteration stops when any of the following holds:

    no eligible SDEcho predicate exists;
    no candidate in the configured pool passes every acceptance gate;
    remaining distance is below a fixed tolerance;
    or the maximum number of iterations is reached.

Previously selected exact events are excluded to prevent cycles.  Nested
predicates are allowed because Country=USA and Country=USA AND
Education=Doctoral can describe different residual structure.  A nested rule
must still add a feasible, positive balance constraint.


13. ORDER DEPENDENCE AND INTERPRETATION
---------------------------------------

The result is a greedy conditional path, not an order-invariant allocation.
The first intervention changes the weighted population and can change later
rankings.  This is intentional: the user asks what matters next after a
specific standardization.

The path must be described as

    first P1;
    conditional on balancing P1, next P2;
    conditional on balancing P1 and P2, next P3.

It must not be described as a unique additive truth that would be unchanged
under every possible ordering.  Stability should be evaluated through repeated
sampling, alternative first-step paths, or held-out validation.


14. SUPPORT, RELIABILITY, AND ASSUMPTIONS
-----------------------------------------

The method assumes:

    the outcome and bucket definition are fixed before discovery;
    each requested event and its complement have adequate raw, active, and
    effective support in both source and target within every intervention
    bucket;
    weighted means are meaningful for the outcome;
    remaining conditional outcomes are retained rather than transported;
    the chosen source-to-target direction is substantively justified.

Each accepted step reports:

    raw predicate support in both groups;
    weighted predicate shares;
    balance error for all prior constraints;
    Kish effective sample size;
    minimum and maximum positive weights;
    distance before balancing;
    distance after removal;
    distance after balancing;
    conditional and cumulative reduction.

Weights create a pseudo-population; they do not create new observed people.
Results are conditional on observed source support and feasibility of the
requested event-margin constraints.  The implementation is event-margin
support-aware; it does not claim complete joint common support across every
selected predicate.  Target-only joint combinations cannot be created by
source weights.  Any future trimming policy must report omitted source and
target mass and define the resulting overlap population explicitly.


15. OUTLIERS AND OUTCOME ROBUSTNESS
-----------------------------------

Mean salary sequences can be dominated by a small upper tail.  In the real
USA 3-5 bucket, medians were near 123000 and 125000 while means were about
378632 and 490669, with multi-million-dollar upper-tail observations.

Therefore, the final empirical evaluation should pre-specify a robust primary
outcome or provide sensitivity analyses using a fixed transformation such as
log salary, global winsorization, trimmed means, or medians.  The transformation
must not be chosen separately after observing each comparison's result.


16. SYNTHETIC GROUND TRUTH
--------------------------

The deterministic positive-control dataset contains four buckets and three
independent binary predicate events:

    P1: Country=USA
    P2: Education=Doctoral
    P3: RemoteWork=Remote.

Source prevalences are 0.10, 0.20, and 0.25.  Target prevalences are 0.30,
0.40, and 0.50.  Conditional salary premiums are identical between groups:

    P1 premium = 60000
    P2 premium = 35000
    P3 premium = 16000.

The target also has an irreducible 2000 shift in every bucket.  Each initial
bucket gap is therefore

    0.20*60000 + 0.20*35000 + 0.25*16000 + 2000
      = 25000.

Across four equal gaps, the exact expected distance path is

    D_0 = 50000
    after P1 balance: D_1 = 26000
    after P2 balance: D_2 = 12000
    after P3 balance: D_3 = 4000.

The original-scale path increments are

    P1: (50000-26000)/50000 = 0.48
    P2: (26000-12000)/50000 = 0.28
    P3: (12000-4000)/50000  = 0.16.

They sum to 0.92, leaving the designed 0.08 residual.  The executable tests
require exact recovery of the predicate order and distance path, preservation
of all earlier constraints, deterministic output, and correct stopping when no
remaining candidate yields a material balance improvement.


17. CLAIM BOUNDARY
------------------

Defensible claim:

    The method constructs an ordered, event-margin-support-aware
    counterfactual explanation path by repeatedly discovering
    removal-influential predicates and standardizing their exact prevalence
    in the source population within the frozen intervention buckets.

Claims that are not justified:

    a predicate causes the sequence gap;
    deletion and balancing estimate the same quantity;
    the greedy order is unique;
    the selected predicates explain unsupported populations;
    or a large removal score is automatically a large compositional share.

The central scientific output is the distinction between removal influence,
prevalence composition, and the remaining residual under the configured
greedy path.  Persistence after prevalence balance is consistent with
conditional-outcome heterogeneity, but can also reflect outliers, correlated
unbalanced structure, and denominator renormalization.

The current real-data path is discovered and accepted on the same sample.  Its
effects are exploratory in-sample descriptive quantities until stability or
held-out evaluation is added.  The historical numerical comparisons in
Section 5 come from the retired one-shot implementation and are retained for
motivation; the active iterative runner does not reproduce every retired
estimand.
